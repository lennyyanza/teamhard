Why air and water?

The design brief, operating requirements, and research behind HARD.

The problem

Built for one medium.
Needed in both.

Aerial and underwater vehicles are usually designed for different operating conditions. Most aerial drones are not designed for submersion, while underwater vehicles are not designed to fly efficiently between locations. Missions like search and rescue, hull and marine infrastructure inspection, and coastal survey need a single platform that can fly to a site, enter the water under its own power, operate submerged, and exit again. Hybrid aerial-underwater vehicles currently exist mostly as research prototypes, with designs focused on transitioning between air and water while balancing speed, stability, and responsiveness.

Proposed approach

A printed body.
Waterproof propulsion.

The vehicle body shall be 3D printed, and its motors shall be waterproof. The current concept should use four motors and four propellers. In air, the motors should operate at high RPM and low torque; underwater, they should operate at low RPM and high torque. The vehicle should include FPGA integration for functionality to be determined later in the project. Simulation and testing should guide the final configuration.

Design requirements

What the vehicle should do.

  • Fly and swim
  • Hover
  • Prioritize stability
  • Transition between air and water
  • Use a 3D-printed body
  • Use waterproof motors
  • Support FPGA integration
  • Support remote control
  • Protect onboard electronics from water
