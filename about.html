01 / About HARD

Across the air–water boundary.

One mission can span two environments. The vehicle should be able to follow.

The problem

Built for one medium.
Needed in both.

Vehicles that operate in air and vehicles that operate underwater are built for one medium and fail in the other. Aerial drones do not survive submersion; underwater vehicles cannot reposition quickly across distance. Missions like search and rescue, hull and marine infrastructure inspection, and coastal survey need a single platform that can fly to a site, enter the water under its own power, operate submerged, and exit again. Hybrid aerial-underwater vehicles exist today mostly as research prototypes, and they generally optimize for survivability of the transition rather than for speed, stability, and responsiveness.

Proposed approach

A shared drive.
A watertight core.

HARD is a tail-sitting hybrid aerial-underwater vehicle built around a watertight, 3D-printed hull and a dual-mode propulsion system. Rather than carrying separate aerial propellers and underwater thrusters, a single motor drives either path depending on rotation direction: clockwise runs the aerial propeller direct-drive at high RPM, counter-clockwise routes through a 12.7:1 reduction for high-torque, low-RPM underwater thrust. A Pixhawk-class flight controller running PX4 handles attitude and rate control in both media, swapping motor mixers on a hydrostatic-pressure submersion flag. An FPGA compute module provides low-latency onboard inference for the responsive behavior the name promises. Development is simulation-first, with physical build gated behind a validated model.
